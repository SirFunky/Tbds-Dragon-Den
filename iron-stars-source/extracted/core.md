⚔️ OBJECTIVE

[core:b0011] 
Defeat your opponent by completing mission objectives, destroying enemy mechs, and seizing control of key locations.

[core:b0012] heading 3
Standard Objective Marker Size

[core:b0013] 
25 mm to 40 mm diameter base, or


[core:b0014] 
2–4 cm token radius (round)

[core:b0015] 
A unit controls the objective if it is within 3 cm of the center and no enemies are also within that range

[core:b0016] 
Optional: Place a mech part, relay tower, or glowing token for visual flair.

[core:b0017] heading 3
🏁 End of Game: Victory & Draws

[core:b0018] 
At the end of the game, the player with the most Victory Points (VP) wins.

[core:b0019] heading 3
🤝 In Case of a Draw (Same VP):

[core:b0020] 
      Use the following tie-breakers in order, unless the mission specifies otherwise:

[core:b0021] 
Control of More Objectives


[core:b0022] 
If one player controls more active objectives, they win.


[core:b0023] 
Total Units Remaining


[core:b0024] 
The player with more surviving units wins.


[core:b0025] 
Total Points of Destroyed Enemy Units


[core:b0026] 
The player who destroyed the most enemy unit points (by cost) wins.


[core:b0027] 
Last to Destroy a Unit


[core:b0028] 
If still tied, the player who last destroyed an enemy unit wins.


[core:b0029] 
Draw Declared


[core:b0030] 
If all else is tied, the game ends in a draw — no victor, just burning mechs.

🌍 TERRAIN & MOVEMENT RULES

[core:b0049] 
Iron Stars uses centimeters (cm) for all distances. Mechs are powerful, but terrain shapes the battle — smart positioning is key.

[core:b0050] 


[core:b0051] heading 3
🚶‍♂️ CORE MOVEMENT RULES

[core:b0052] 
A unit may move up to its M value (in cm) per Move action


[core:b0053] 
Mechs may move through friendly units, but not end movement on top of them


[core:b0054] 
Units may pivot freely without costing movement

[core:b0057] TABLE

core:b0057:r3 | Difficult Ground (mud, snow, debris) | -5 cm movement | None

[core:b0058] 


[core:b0059] heading 3
🛡️ COVER RULE (Recap)

[core:b0060] 
If a unit is partially obscured by terrain from the attacker’s point of view, it is in Cover


[core:b0061] 
Cover provides +1 Defence vs ranged attacks


[core:b0062] 
Cover does not apply to melee attacks


[core:b0063] 


[core:b0064] heading 3
🔧 Wrecks as Terrain

[core:b0065] 
When a mech is destroyed, do not remove it from the table. Instead, leave the model in place as a wreck.

[core:b0066] heading 3
🧩 Wreck Rules:

[core:b0067] 
Counts as Impassable Terrain (cannot move through or stand on)


[core:b0068] 
Provides Cover (+1 Defence vs ranged attacks) to units within 2.5 cm of it


[core:b0069] 
Does not block line of sight, but may obscure it (use model silhouette)


[core:b0070] 
Wrecks can’t be targeted, but may be removed in narrative or campaign missions


[core:b0071] heading 3
🧠 Optional Advanced Rule:

[core:b0072] 
Wreckage is Hazardous — units ending their turn in base contact take 1 STR damage (e.g. overheating reactor, unstable frame)


[core:b0073] 
Engineer units may salvage a wreck for repair or drone deployment

[core:b0095] heading 2
🎲 COMBAT

[core:b0096] heading 3
Attacking

[core:b0097] 
Choose a weapon and a valid target


[core:b0098] 
Roll a number of D6 equal to the weapon’s FP (ranged) or ME (melee)


[core:b0099] 
Compare each die to the target’s Defence (D)


[core:b0100] 
Each die equal to or higher than the target's Defence = 1 hit


[core:b0101] 
Each hit = 1 Structure damage (unless modified)


[core:b0102] 


[core:b0103] heading 3
🎯 Natural Dice Rule

[core:b0104] 
A natural 6 always hits, even if the target's Defence is higher than the attacker's roll modifiers


[core:b0105] 
A natural 1 always misses, even if modifiers would normally make the hit succeed

[core:b0106] 


[core:b0107] heading 3
Weapon Format

[core:b0108] 
Ranged Weapon:
 FP | Range | Keywords


[core:b0109] 
Melee Weapon:
 ME | Keywords

[core:b0110] 
Melee Restriction:

[core:b0111] 
Ranged weapons (FP) cannot be used in melee combat.

[core:b0112] 
Only melee weapons (ME) and melee-based systems may be used in close combat.

[core:b0113] 
If a unit is engaged in melee, it may only attack with its ME stat or melee systems.

[core:b0114] 
Example:

[core:b0115] 
Rail Carbine: FP 2 | Range: 45.5 cm | Piercing 1, Accurate


[core:b0116] 
Beam Saber: ME 3 | AP 1, Cleave


[core:b0117] 


[core:b0118] 
Weapon Use Restriction

[core:b0119] 
Each weapon system may only be used once per activation.


[core:b0120] 
A unit with multiple weapons may use each of them once (spending an action for each).


[core:b0121] 
If a weapon has the Rapid Fire keyword or another special rule, it will state its own exceptions.

[core:b0122] 


[core:b0123] heading 2
🧭 ACTION LIST

[core:b0124] 
Each unit can take 2 actions per activation:

[core:b0125] 
Move – Move up to M in cm.


[core:b0126] 
Attack – Make a ranged or melee attack.


[core:b0127] 
Transform – Switch between forms (if applicable).


[core:b0128] 
Use System/Ability – Activate a system or trait.


[core:b0129] 
Braced – Gain +1 Defense until the start of the unit’s next activation. The unit may not Move during the same activation. Braced ends if the unit Moves (voluntary or forced).


[core:b0130] 
Interact – Perform an interaction with terrain or scenario objectives.

[core:b0131] 


[core:b0132] heading 2
💥 DAMAGE & DESTRUCTION

[core:b0133] 
Each hit = 1 Structure lost


[core:b0134] 
At 0 Structure, the unit is destroyed

[core:b0135] 
Delayed Destruction

[core:b0136] 
Units reduced to 0 STR remain on the battlefield until the end of the current round. They may complete their current activation if they have not already acted. This allows units to strike back or complete objectives before becoming a wreck. A destroyed unit cant contest or interact with objectives.

🪖 COVER & MODIFIERS

[core:b0139]

[core:b0141] heading 2
🚨 OPTIONAL MODULE: MORALE & SUPPRESSION

[core:b0142] heading 3
Trigger a Morale Test when:

[core:b0143] 
The unit takes damage and survives


[core:b0144] 
An allied unit within 15.0 cm is destroyed


[core:b0145] heading 3
Morale Test:

[core:b0146] 
Roll 1D6
 If the result is less than current STR, the unit becomes Suppressed

[core:b0147] heading 3
Suppressed Units:

[core:b0148] 
Can only take 1 action on next activation


[core:b0149] 
Cannot charge


[core:b0150] heading 3
Modifiers:

[core:b0151] 
In cover: +1 to morale roll


[core:b0152] 
Near commander (within 15.0 cm): Reroll failed morale


[core:b0153] 
Drones/AI: Immune

🪖 Faction Profile: The Living Dominion

[core:b0524] 
“The flesh remembers. The land endures.”

[core:b0525] heading 2
🌍 Overview

[core:b0526] 
The Living Dominion is not a nation in the conventional sense, nor a single people with one capital, one language, or one throne. It is a vast nomadic civilization spread across most of Africa, formed from many older peoples now bound together by a shared way of life known as The Green Path.

[core:b0527] 
Where other powers build cities, factories, and fixed borders, the Living Dominion moves in scattered circles that remain in constant communion through living networks of root, spore, beast, and blood. To outsiders, it is a land of vanished expeditions, broken invasions, and impossible monsters. To its own people, it is simply life defending itself.

[core:b0528] 
Its most feared guardians are the Warforms — colossal living war-bodies grown, guided, and inhabited by chosen Wardens. These beings are not mindless beasts, nor mere biological machines, but sacred weapons of the Dominion’s will. When foreign armies push too far inland, strip too much from the land, or mistake distance for safety, the Warforms come.

[core:b0529] 
Few who meet them survive.

[core:b0530] 


[core:b0531] heading 2
🧠 Philosophy & Society

[core:b0532] 
Many Peoples, One Path:
 The Dominion is made up of many cultures, lineages, and local traditions, united not by uniformity but by shared survival, mutual duty, and reverence for living systems.

[core:b0533] 
The Green Path:
 The Dominion’s guiding philosophy teaches that life endures through balance, adaptation, and cultivation — not domination, extraction, or sterile control.

[core:b0534] 
Movement Over Permanence:
 Most of the Dominion lives in small roaming circles rather than permanent cities. These groups travel constantly, tending routes, sacred sites, relay-groves, and living camps.

[core:b0535] 
Biology, Not Industry:
 The Dominion is not primitive. It has simply advanced along another path. Its communication, medicine, shelter, and warcraft are grown, cultivated, and symbiotic rather than mechanical.

[core:b0536] 


[core:b0537] heading 2
⚔️ Military Doctrine

[core:b0538] 
Territory Denial Through Ambush:
 The Living Dominion rarely fights as a conventional front-line state. It lets invaders stretch themselves thin, then strikes with overwhelming force once they are exposed.

[core:b0539] 
Warforms as Apex Weapons:
 The Warforms are living engines of war — massive, regenerative, and terrifyingly difficult to put down permanently.

[core:b0540] 
Decentralized Warfare:
 The Dominion has no single command center to destroy. Its scattered circles and Wild Hosts can lose ground, vanish, regroup, and strike elsewhere with little warning.

[core:b0541] 
Living Infrastructure:
 The same biotech principles that sustain Dominion life also sustain Dominion war. Signals travel through fungal rootwebs, wounds are treated with cultivated organisms, and support systems are alive as surely as the Warforms themselves.

[core:b0542] 


[core:b0543] heading 2
🧬 Core Faction Rule: Renewal of Flesh

[core:b0544] 
At the end of each round, before removing destroyed Warforms, roll 1D6 for each friendly Warform that is damaged or destroyed and still represented on the battlefield. On a 6, that Warform regains 1 STR, to a maximum of its starting STR. If a destroyed Warform regains STR this way, it is no longer destroyed, stands up with 1 STR, and becomes Suppressed. A Warform restored by Renewal of Flesh is not treated as a wreck.

[core:b0545] 


[core:b0546] heading 2
🧭 Subfactions – The Four Wild Hosts

[core:b0547] 
Unlike the subfactions of other powers, the Wild Hosts do not represent rigid political divisions or formal standing armies. They are vast roaming coalitions of Wardens, circles, and Warforms shaped by different histories, frontiers, and philosophies of war.
 All Living Dominion forces use the faction rule Renewal of Flesh rather than separate Wild Host traits.

[core:b0548] heading 3
The World-Treaders

[core:b0549] 
The largest and heaviest of the Wild Hosts.

[core:b0550] 
Their Warforms are vast, brutal, and built for direct territorial denial. When an invasion must be broken by raw force, the World-Treaders answer with enormous living engines that crash through gunlines and fortified positions alike.

[core:b0551] heading 3
The Crown of Ash

[core:b0552] 
Ancient, saurian, and deeply feared even within the Dominion.

[core:b0553] 
This Host fields Warforms that seem less like grown weapons and more like living catastrophes dredged from the world’s oldest memory. Slow, terrible, and relentless, they embody the wrath of the deep earth.

[core:b0554] heading 3
The Veiled Pulse

[core:b0555] 
Fast, precise, and deeply unsettling.

[core:b0556] 
Its Warforms are more humanoid, more controlled, and more uncanny than those of the other Hosts. Many within the Dominion distrust their methods, fearing they stand too close to a path that should never have been walked — yet their loyalty and battlefield value are beyond dispute.

[core:b0557] heading 3
The Ironroot Host

[core:b0558] 
The most divisive of the Wild Hosts.

[core:b0559] 
Where others reject dead machinery, the Ironroot Host is willing to graft, bind, and repurpose limited technological remnants into living flesh. Many see them as dangerously compromised. Others see them as practical defenders willing to use any tool that still serves life.

[core:b0560] 


[core:b0561] heading 2
🤖 Unit Example: Prowler Warform

[core:b0562] 
The most widespread Warform pattern of the Living Dominion.

[core:b0563] 
A mid-sized apex predator bred for pursuit, ambush, and brutal close assault, the Prowler Warform is the shape most invaders encounter first. It lacks the monumental scale of the World-Treaders and the mythic grandeur of the Crown of Ash, but it is faster, more adaptable, and common across all four Wild Hosts.

[core:b0564] 
Its silhouette is low, muscular, and predatory, with heavy forelimbs, layered biological armor, and a cultivated hunting instinct directed by the Warden within. The Prowler is not a sacred relic or rare terror-beast. It is the Dominion’s most practical answer to trespass.

[core:b0565]

🌍 Faction Profile: Southern Cross Compact

[core:b0580] 
“We’ll fight in anything that still walks — for the right price.”

[core:b0581] heading 3
🌍 Overview

[core:b0582] 
The Southern Cross Compact is not a nation, a corporation, or a single army. It is a loose coalition of Australian mercenary companies, salvage syndicates, transport guilds, security contractors, and battlefield recovery crews bound together by contracts, reputation, and necessity.

[core:b0583] 
Where the great powers build doctrines, the Compact builds invoices.

[core:b0584] 
Their forces are made up of stock mechs bought, leased, salvaged, or captured from the battlefields of the Iron Concord, UN Mega Corporations, and New Avalon. They lack the deep industrial base and technical mastery of the great powers, but make up for it with practical experience, local knowledge, and a culture built around keeping old war machines running one contract at a time.

[core:b0585] 
The Southern Cross Compact does not invent the best mechs in the world.

[core:b0586] 
It simply knows how to get them, field them, and survive inside them.

[core:b0587] 


[core:b0588] heading 3

[core:b0589] 
Contract Before Creed: Loyalty lasts as long as the job, the payout, and the odds.

[core:b0590] 
Coalition of Companies: The Compact is made up of many smaller firms and warbands, not a single central state.

[core:b0591] 
Salvage Culture: Nothing is wasted if it can still move, shoot, or be sold for parts.

[core:b0592] 
Pragmatic Warfare: Compact pilots value survival, mobility, and battlefield utility over ideology or honor.

[core:b0600] heading 3
🧬 Core Faction Rule: FIELD EXPEDIENTS

[core:b0601] 
Once per round, when a Southern Cross Compact unit would suffer a terrain-based movement penalty, you may ignore that penalty for that unit.

[core:b0602] 
This may ignore penalties such as:

[core:b0603] 
Difficult Ground movement reduction


[core:b0604] 
Ruins / Rubble movement reduction


[core:b0605] 
Forest / Light Cover movement reduction


[core:b0606] 
Obstacle vault costs


[core:b0607] 
Elevation climbing costs


[core:b0608] 
Field Expedients does not ignore effects that prevent movement entirely unless a rule specifically states they are a terrain penalty.

[core:b0609] 


[core:b0610] heading 3

[core:b0611] 
The Southern Cross Compact does not use its own dedicated mech roster in the normal way. Instead, it fields eligible stock mechs hired or acquired from other powers.

[core:b0612] 
A Southern Cross Compact force may include non-Hero, non-Unique stock mechs from:

[core:b0613] 
Iron Concord


[core:b0614] 
UN Mega Corporations


[core:b0615] 
New Avalon


[core:b0616] 
A Southern Cross Compact force may never include units from the Cybernetic Rebellion.

[core:b0617] 


[core:b0618] heading 3
🔒 IMPORT RESTRICTIONS

[core:b0619] 
When a Southern Cross Compact force includes an imported mech, apply all of the following rules:

[core:b0620] 
1. Stock Mechs Only
 Imported mechs must be taken as complete, stock units exactly as listed on their source faction roster.

[core:b0621] 
2. No Customization
 Imported mechs may not change their frame, weapons, or systems. They may not purchase alternate faction options, replacement loadouts, or additional armory upgrades unless those options are explicitly part of their stock unit entry.

[core:b0622] 
3. No Heroes or Named Units
 The Southern Cross Compact may not include Heroes, named characters, or other Unique faction centerpiece units from another faction.

[core:b0623] 
4. Keep the Mech, Lose the Doctrine
 Imported mechs keep their printed weapons, systems, and unit rules on their stock profile, but do not gain:

[core:b0624] 
source faction core rules


[core:b0625] 
subfaction traits


[core:b0626] 
House, Order, Sponsor, or equivalent detachment bonuses


[core:b0627] 
faction-wide command benefits


[core:b0628] 
any bonus rule granted for belonging to their original faction as an army


[core:b0629] 
5. Faction Identity Changes
 For all army-building and game purposes, imported mechs count as Southern Cross Compact units, not as units of their original faction.

[core:b0630] 
6. Source-Locked Benefits Are Lost
 If a rule only functions because the unit belongs to Iron Concord, UNMC, or New Avalon as a faction, that part of the rule is ignored in a Southern Cross Compact force.

[core:b0631]

[core:b0667] heading 3
🎯 Tactical Guidance

[core:b0668] 
Use Iron Concord imports when you need rugged, cost-efficient line units.

[core:b0669] 
Use UNMC imports when you need mobility, utility, or specialized systems.

[core:b0670]

[core:b0671]
