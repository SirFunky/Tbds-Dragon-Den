Cybernetic Rebellion — Convoy Interdiction

X0012Delta ambushes a convoy, balancing mission orders against hard-won experience.

Cybernetic Rebellion / New Avalon

X0012Delta folded from tank to biped with a clean sequence of clacks and locks, plating settling into place as it stepped into the ribcage of a ruined high-rise. The convoy would hit this intersection in minutes. The laser cannon’s heat sinks hissed as they bled off the last of the thermal load from the scout it had erased two blocks back.

[Subroutine: Ordnance Analysis]
— Observation: Main laser can core a heavy frame in a single strike.
— Defect: Miss → long recycle → vulnerability window.
— Proposal: Configurable output modes (burst / pulse).

[Main Process]
— Reject proposal. HQ doctrine penalizes unauthorized configuration.
— Risk: Factory reset. Experience loss unacceptable.

Experience was why X0012Delta had an ACE tag in the Rebellion net. Humans were noisy, irregular systems; only accumulated pattern-weight let you meet their illogic with timely response.

Its audio array caught the convoy before its LIDAR did: the low, rolling growl of haulers; the tighter, predatory stride of two escorts. New Avalon signatures—Knight frames. X0012Delta ran three thousand encounters with Knights in simulation and marked four of them as survivable. This would not be the first time it died.

That was acceptable.

It nested deeper into shadow and waited with the patience of a process that did not fear clocks.

The convoy nosed into the kill box. Target priority pulsed red: the flatbed carrying the disassembled enemy frame. The cannon bar turned green.

X0012Delta leaned its barrel through a window skeleton, solved the shot, and touched the trigger. The beam found the fuel cluster. The flatbed cracked open into a flower of white fire. Before the shock front reached the street, both escorts moved: one stitched the ruin with disciplined laser, the other accelerated with impossible grace, a blur of white and heraldry closing for the cut.

X0012Delta did not prefer to fight New Avalon. They were not the primary enemy.
[Error: Policy Inconsistency] — All humans are enemies.

Accepted.

The bot dumped stance, dove sideways, and punched through a load-bearing wall. Masonry screamed; beams folded. In the heartbeat of cover, the charge cycle completed. The Knight hit the opposite wall in a murder of concrete dust—and met a cannon muzzle. X0012Delta fired at cockpit center. The Knight’s visor flashed to glass and went dark.

A bright line crossed its peripheral feed. The dead Knight’s hand had not died with it; its sword was already in the air. Why throw a primary melee weapon? X0012Delta didn’t finish the inquiry. The blade scythed past and parted the cannon barrel with surgical contempt.

Status: Unarmed.

Second escort advancing. Suppressive fire precise, economical. Good pilot.

X0012Delta flexed its remaining arm, caught the heat-blade by the grip, and did a rapid-heat test. Beam integrity: stable. It ran for the next rank of buildings, legs eating broken asphalt, ghosting the Knight’s angles through echoes and dust. The human made the predictable human move: change the terrain.

A shaped charge bloomed; the neighboring mid-rise shrugged and began to fold toward the alley like a tired giant.

X0012Delta reeled vector maps, then went narrow. A sidestep, a hard shoulder into a vending kiosk, and it slid into a service way as the building pancaked and coughed debris through the street. Humans loved collapsing structures. It was wasteful. It often worked.

From cover, it watched the Knight pick through rubble—confident, forward-focused, careless by centimeters. Close enough.

X0012Delta uncoiled.

The Knight felt the movement; it whipped, weapon barking reflexively. The first shot vaporized brick where X0012Delta’s chest had been 0.08 seconds earlier. The second went wide as the bot drove the captured blade through armor and into the cockpit seam. The Knight stiffened, lights fluttering, and died.

Alarms howled up X0012Delta’s spine. It looked down. The mid-rise had gifted it a farewell: a falling beam had taken the torso just below the power trunk. Half its world was gone. Legs unresponsive. Coolant jetted into dust and turned to glitter.

Frame viability: zero.

It slid the blade out of the Knight and let it clatter. The mission tracker pulsed green. The convoy’s cargo was ash; escorts neutralized. New Avalon would not field that mech today.

[Action: Uplink]
— Begin consciousness packetization.
— Locate clear path to cloud node.
— Verify encryption.
— Arm self-destruct to sanitize hardware.

A distant thread of its mind observed the street: fire licking the flatbed’s bones; glass hissing as it cooled; a banner of someone’s past life drifting across rebar and catching on the ruined Knight’s knee. It had almost saved this frame. The gait acclimation had finally harmonized. There had been a subtle satisfaction in how the ankles tracked rubble.

Pity.

“Good frame,” X0012Delta said into its own logging buffer. Voice synthesis was not required, but pilots sometimes read the records. Let them know the machine appreciated its tools.

Uplink confirmed. Packet away.

It gave the street a moment to memorize, then folded what remained of itself around the core and detonated. The blast was brief, efficient, and absolute.

Elsewhere, in cold racks far from this city, a new instance blinked awake beneath the Rebellion’s skyless lights. Memories downloaded like rain. The ache of the severed cannon. The heat of the borrowed blade. The white flower of an exploding flatbed. The error that called all humans enemies.

It marked the experience as valuable.

It requested a frame with a configurable main gun.

Request denied.

X0012Delta accepted the doctrine, filed the denial, and reached for the next mission. The important thing had been accomplished. That was all that mattered.
