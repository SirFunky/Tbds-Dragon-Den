Background

Background

The Southern Cross Compact is not a nation, a corporation, or a single army. It is a loose coalition of Australian mercenary companies, salvage syndicates, transport guilds, security contractors, and battlefield recovery crews bound together by contracts, reputation, and necessity.

Where the great powers build doctrines, the Compact builds invoices.

Their forces are made up of stock mechs bought, leased, salvaged, or captured from the battlefields of the Iron Concord, UN Mega Corporations, and New Avalon. They lack the deep industrial base and technical mastery of the great powers, but make up for it with practical experience, local knowledge, and a culture built around keeping old war machines running one contract at a time.

The Southern Cross Compact does not invent the best mechs in the world.

It simply knows how to get them, field them, and survive inside them.

Ideology

Beliefs & society

Contract Before Creed: Loyalty lasts as long as the job, the payout, and the odds.

Coalition of Companies: The Compact is made up of many smaller firms and warbands, not a single central state.

Salvage Culture: Nothing is wasted if it can still move, shoot, or be sold for parts.

Pragmatic Warfare: Compact pilots value survival, mobility, and battlefield utility over ideology or honor.

Doctrine

Military doctrine

Stock Machine Procurement: Compact forces field whole imported mechs, not custom hybrid rebuilds.

Flexible Hiring Pool: Able to cherry-pick stock machines from several rival factions.

Terrain Savvy: Veteran crews know how to move through harsh ground, ruins, and improvised battlefields.

No Native Doctrine: Compact units do not benefit from the faction-wide military doctrines of the powers they buy from.

Faction rules

🧬 Core Faction Rule: FIELD EXPEDIENTS

Once per round, when a Southern Cross Compact unit would suffer a terrain-based movement penalty, you may ignore that penalty for that unit.

This may ignore penalties such as:

Difficult Ground movement reduction

Ruins / Rubble movement reduction

Forest / Light Cover movement reduction

Obstacle vault costs

Elevation climbing costs

Field Expedients does not ignore effects that prevent movement entirely unless a rule specifically states they are a terrain penalty.

📦 CONTRACT PROCUREMENT

The Southern Cross Compact does not use its own dedicated mech roster in the normal way. Instead, it fields eligible stock mechs hired or acquired from other powers.

A Southern Cross Compact force may include non-Hero, non-Unique stock mechs from:

Iron Concord

UN Mega Corporations

New Avalon

A Southern Cross Compact force may never include units from the Cybernetic Rebellion.

🔒 IMPORT RESTRICTIONS

When a Southern Cross Compact force includes an imported mech, apply all of the following rules:

1. Stock Mechs Only
Imported mechs must be taken as complete, stock units exactly as listed on their source faction roster.

2. No Customization
Imported mechs may not change their frame, weapons, or systems. They may not purchase alternate faction options, replacement loadouts, or additional armory upgrades unless those options are explicitly part of their stock unit entry.

3. No Heroes or Named Units
The Southern Cross Compact may not include Heroes, named characters, or other Unique faction centerpiece units from another faction.

4. Keep the Mech, Lose the Doctrine
Imported mechs keep their printed weapons, systems, and unit rules on their stock profile, but do not gain:

source faction core rules

subfaction traits

House, Order, Sponsor, or equivalent detachment bonuses

faction-wide command benefits

any bonus rule granted for belonging to their original faction as an army

5. Faction Identity Changes
For all army-building and game purposes, imported mechs count as Southern Cross Compact units, not as units of their original faction.

6. Source-Locked Benefits Are Lost
If a rule only functions because the unit belongs to Iron Concord, UNMC, or New Avalon as a faction, that part of the rule is ignored in a Southern Cross Compact force.

💰 IMPORT TAX

When including an imported mech in a Southern Cross Compact force, add both a Chassis Tax and a Source Modifier to that mech’s final points cost.

Chassis Tax

Light: +2 pts

Medium: +3 pts

Heavy: +4 pts

Superheavy / Elite: +5 pts

Source Modifier

Iron Concord: +1 pt

UNMC: +3 pts

New Avalon: +5 pts

These modifiers are cumulative.

Examples

Iron Concord Light Mech: +3 pts

Iron Concord Heavy Mech: +5 pts

UNMC Medium Mech: +6 pts

New Avalon Heavy Mech: +9 pts

New Avalon Elite Mech: +10 pts

🚫 SOUTHERN CROSS COMPACT LIMITATIONS

The Southern Cross Compact has unusual access to other factions’ machines, but pays for that flexibility with real limitations.

Strengths

Can cherry-pick stock mechs from multiple factions

Strong list-building flexibility

Reliable terrain utility through Field Expedients

Weaknesses

No Cybernetic Rebellion access

No imported Heroes or Unique units

No faction-wide source bonuses

No mech customization

Imported prestige units become expensive quickly

Needs clarification

The Compact uses imported stock mechs rather than a native roster. The source gives no formal subfaction list. Chassis-tier tax mappings and eligibility of House/Order-exclusive imports still need confirmation; no eligible-unit list or replacement costs have been guessed.

🎯 Tactical Guidance

Use Iron Concord imports when you need rugged, cost-efficient line units.

Use UNMC imports when you need mobility, utility, or specialized systems.

Use New Avalon imports sparingly as elite prestige picks — they are powerful, but the import cost rises quickly.

Do not try to out-doctrine the original factions. The Compact wins by bringing the right stock machine to the right contract, not by matching the parent faction at its own specialty.

🪙 Optional Narrative Note: Mercenary Companies

Players are encouraged to name their company, color their machines by contract origin, and invent histories of who bought what from whom.
